### [AI Detection Is About Who Holds Power](https://seangoedecke.com/who-gets-to-decide-if-something-is-written-by-ai/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 27, 00:00

**「Background」** Disputes over whether a prize-winning story, a contracted manuscript, or a tweet was AI-generated keep raising the same question: who gets to decide? Sean Goedecke argues that arguments about detector reliability or the ethics of scanning writing sit downstream of a deeper conflict over authority.

**「Solution」** Goedecke contends that the real issue is power—who may gatekeep literary prizes, fail students, or exclude people from public discourse. He sympathetically reconstructs the anti-detection position: if AI concentrates social power in the tech sector, making another tech company’s tool like Pangram the arbiter is not reassuring. Skeptics also distrust “tool-shaped” solutions generally, seeing legible software processes replace messy but human community judgment. The author disagrees because the only alternative is community self-policing, which he considers worse: people who avoid AI are poor at recognizing its tells and often misclassify long, polite human writing as AI. Ordinary investigation cannot settle skilled cases either, since AI writing can have plausible drafts and contemporaneous discussions, and there is no practical cryptographic attestation path. If the goal is keeping AI out of human cultural spaces, he concludes, advocates should use the strongest detection tools available—ideally open-source or government-built—rather than leave authorship judgments to the crowd.

**「Takeaway」** For Goedecke, the detection debate is less about whether a classifier is accurate than about who should exercise the authority to define authorship; relying on community intuition, he argues, would both misjudge individuals and let more AI-generated work through.
